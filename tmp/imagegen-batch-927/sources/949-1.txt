Such curves are sometimes also known as rollers. The &quot;width&quot; of a closed convex curve is defined as the distance between parallel lines bounding it (&quot;supporting lines&quot;). Every curve of constant width is convex. Curves of constant width have the same &quot;width&quot; regardless of their orientation between the parallel lines. In fact, they also share the same perimeter ( Barbier's theorem ). Examples include the circle (with largest area ), and Reuleaux triangle (with smallest area ) but there are an infinite number. A curve of constant width can be used in a special drill chuck to cut square &quot; holes .&quot; A generalization gives solids of constant width. These do not have the same surface area for a given width, but their shadows are curves of constant width with the same width!
