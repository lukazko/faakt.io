These themes examine how Christie destabilizes narrative authority by revealing that truth is always mediated through perspective and memory, and shaped by interpretation. In Roger Ackroyd and Five Little Pigs , narration itself becomes suspect, forcing readers to confront the limits of certainty and the fragility of trust. Christie transforms storytelling into a philosophical problem which examines the reliability of the structures through which evidence is communicated.
